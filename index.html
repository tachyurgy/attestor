The weakest link in enterprise identity is the service desk. An attacker calls claiming to be a locked-out employee, applies time pressure, and asks a human to reset a second factor — and the human is the control. This models the replacement: evidence scored into an assurance ledger, weighed against the blast radius of what is being asked, with attack indicators that subtract rather than merely annotate. The passport check digits are computed for real.

How it is built. mrz.js implements ICAO 9303 TD3 parsing and check-digit validation exactly — the 7-3-1 weight cycle, A=10..Z=35 letter mapping, and the composite digit spanning four fields. The tampered document in the second session is generated by taking a valid MRZ and changing one digit, which is what makes it a genuine test rather than a hardcoded “fail” flag. policy.js scores assurance against a required level per action, and treats any high-severity injection indicator as disqualifying rather than merely negative.

That last rule is the substantive design opinion here. Assurance signals are additive only while you can trust the capture chain; an attacker running a virtual camera can manufacture unlimited passing biometric checks, so summing more of them proves nothing. All identities, documents and sessions are fabricated.
